The Eight Wonders of the world


Knowing The Wonderous monuments


Taj Mahal

The Taj Mahal is an iconic ivory-white marble mausoleum located on the southern bank of the Yamuna River in Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India. Commissioned in 1631 by the fifth Mughal Emperor, Shah Jahan, it was built to house the tomb of his favourite wife, Mumtaz Mahal, and later became the resting place for the Emperor himself. Recognized globally as a universal masterpiece and an eternal symbol of love, it was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983 and stands as one of the New Eight Wonders of the World.

Great Walls of China

The Great Wall of China is a colossal series of ancient fortifications, ramparts, and trenches stretching across northern China. Rather than a single continuous line, it is a vast network of defensive structures built, rebuilt, and expanded over two millennia across high mountains, deserts, and grasslands. It is universally recognized as one of the greatest engineering feats in human history and stands as a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the New Eight Wonders of the World.

Petra

The Ancient City of Petra is a world-famous archaeological site located in Jordan's southwestern desert. Originally known to its inhabitants as Raqmu, it served as the thriving capital of the Nabataean Empire between 400 BC and AD 106. Famously nicknamed the "Rose City" due to the striking pink, red, and orange hues of the sandstone cliffs into which it was carved, Petra was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985 and is celebrated as one of the New Eight Wonders of the World.

Machu Picchu

The Ancient Citadel of Machu Picchu is a 15th-century Incan estate located high in the Andes Mountains of southern Peru. Situated on a narrow mountain ridge 2,430 metres (7,970 feet) above sea level, it overlooks the deep Urubamba River valley. Built at the absolute peak of the Inca Empire under the direction of Emperor Pachacuti around 1450, it was abandoned just a century later during the Spanish Conquest. Because the Spanish never discovered it, the site remained exceptionally preserved and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983 and voted one of the New Eight Wonders of the World.

Christ The Redeemer

The Christ the Redeemer statue (Cristo Redentor) is a colossal Art Deco statue of Jesus Christ situated at the summit of Corcovado Mountain in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. Standing 30 metres (98 feet) tall atop an 8-metre pedestal, with arms stretching 28 metres (92 feet) wide, it looks out over the entire city, coastline, and bay. Completed in 1931 after nearly a decade of construction, the monument has become an iconic global symbol of Christianity, peace, and Brazilian culture, earning its status as one of the New Eight Wonders of the World.

The Roman Colosseum

The Roman Colosseum (originally known as the Flavian Amphitheatre) is a massive oval amphitheatre located in the absolute center of Rome, Italy. Built out of concrete, brick, and quarried travertine stone, construction began under Emperor Vespasian in AD 72 and was completed under his heir, Titus, in AD 80. It stands as the largest ancient amphitheatre ever constructed, a flawless masterpiece of Roman engineering, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and one of the New Eight Wonders of the World.

Chichen Itza

The Pre-Columbian City of Chichen Itza was a massive, thriving metropolis built by the Maya civilization in the North-Central region of Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula. Active between roughly AD 600 and 1200, it served as a major regional capital and a sacred center for trade, politics, and religious pilgrimage. Celebrated for its unique fusion of Mayan and Toltec architectural styles, it was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1988 and voted one of the New Eight Wonders of the World.

The Great Pyramid of Giza

The Great Pyramid of Giza (also known as the Pyramid of Khufu) is the oldest and largest of the three pyramids in the Giza pyramid complex bordering Cairo, Egypt. Built around 2560 BC during the Fourth Dynasty of the Old Kingdom, it served as a monumental tomb for the Pharaoh Khufu. It holds a unique dual crown: it is the only surviving member of the original Seven Wonders of the Ancient World and was officially designated an honorary Eighth Wonder of the World during the 2007 global campaign.
